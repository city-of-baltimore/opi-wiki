# Leadership Norms

{{ page_header(summary="What OPI staff should be able to expect from people who supervise or direct their work.") }}

These norms describe how OPI leaders should set priorities, make decisions,
give feedback, and handle concerns. They are guidance, not a disciplinary
policy, employment contract, or alternate management process.

City policy, Civil Service requirements, applicable law, official forms, and
collective bargaining agreements control whenever they differ from this page.
Human Resources and labor relations must confirm any use of these norms in a
formal employment decision.

## Set priorities people can use

Name the result, owner, decision-maker, and main constraint. Explain what comes
first and what can wait. When new work becomes urgent, identify which earlier
commitment should stop, move, or receive less attention.

## Give enough context

Explain why the work matters, who will use it, and what decision or service it
supports. Be clear about which parts are fixed and where staff may use their
judgment.

## Match commitments to capacity

Before promising work to another office or leader, confirm the owner, available
time, and required support. Do not make routine success depend on unpaid work,
constant urgency, skipped review, or individual heroics.

## Make it safe to raise problems

Ask what may fail, what evidence is weak, and what support is missing. Examine
an early warning instead of punishing the person who named it. A leader does
not need to agree with every concern, but should explain what will happen next.

## Make and explain decisions

Say who will decide and when or in what sequence. Invite useful input before
the decision when there is room to change it. After deciding, explain the
choice, its effect, and any open question. Record consequential decisions where
the people doing the work can find them.

## Give timely, specific feedback

Describe the work or behavior, its effect, and what should continue or change.
Give feedback while the person can still use it. Do not substitute hints,
gossip, or accumulated frustration for a direct conversation.

Formal performance action must follow the current City and
bargaining-agreement process.

## Remove barriers leaders control

Help resolve unclear authority, conflicting priorities, missing access, slow
approvals, or resource gaps. Do not label a structural barrier as a staff
member's lack of initiative. If the barrier cannot be removed, explain the
constraint and adjust the plan.

## Apply expectations fairly

Use relevant facts and consider the person's role, authority, access, workload,
accommodations, and changing priorities. Do not hold someone responsible for an
expectation that was not communicated or a decision they could not make.

## Share credit and support growth

Name the people who contributed. Give staff access to useful feedback,
assignments, and learning opportunities. Do not reserve visible work or
recognition for people who are already closest to leadership.

## Protect leave and reasonable workloads

Plan coverage for approved leave. Revisit scope when the available time or
staffing changes.

## Handle conflict responsibly

Address a work problem with the people involved when that is appropriate and
safe. Direct conversation is not required when there is a power, safety,
discrimination, retaliation, legal, or other protected concern. In those cases,
help the person reach the proper route and do not interfere with access.

## Own mistakes

When a leadership decision causes harm, delay, or confusion, acknowledge it,
correct the record, and explain what will change. Holding authority includes
taking responsibility for the conditions in which staff work.

## When leadership falls short

Staff may raise a concern with the leader, another manager, or the next level
of management if they choose. Depending on the issue, they may instead go
directly to Human Resources, labor relations, Equal Employment Opportunity,
Ethics, workplace safety, a union representative, a whistleblower channel, or
another applicable formal route.

A leader must not require manager-first reporting when another route is
available. These norms do not shorten a deadline, limit a right, or change any
protection against retaliation provided by law, policy, or a bargaining
agreement.
