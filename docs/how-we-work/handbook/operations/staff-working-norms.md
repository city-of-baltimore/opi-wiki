# Staff Working Norms

{{ page_header(summary="Ten everyday habits that help OPI staff work clearly and reliably together.") }}

These norms describe everyday habits that make work clear, respectful, and
reliable. They are not a rating system or disciplinary policy. They do not
require people to work outside assigned hours, ignore accessibility needs, move
faster than safety allows, or accept work without enough time and support.

City policy, applicable law, and collective bargaining agreements control.

## 1. Start with the result

Before doing substantial work, be able to say what needs to change, who needs
it, and what decision or action it should support. Ask when the purpose is
unclear.

## 2. Make the next owner clear

Name the person responsible for the next action. Several people may help, but
"the team" is not enough when follow-through matters.

## 3. Keep one shared copy

When people are working from the same document, tracker, or plan, choose one
City-approved shared copy and link to it. Avoid parallel versions unless there
is a clear reason and a plan to bring them back together.

## 4. Write down decisions

Record important decisions where the people doing the work can find them. Say
who decided, what changes, and what remains open. If the decision changes,
update the record and explain why.

## 5. Raise blockers early

Say when access is missing, evidence is weak, a date may move, or two priorities
conflict. Early notice gives people choices. You do not need a complete solution
before raising a real problem.

## 6. Be direct and respectful

Use plain language. Address the work and its effect without attacking the
person. Ask questions before assuming intent. Disagreement is allowed;
dismissiveness, threats, discrimination, and retaliation are not.

## 7. Check work before sharing it

Match the check to the consequence. Verify the facts and details that people
will rely on, and ask for another review when the risk or subject requires it.
If the work is incomplete, say what kind of feedback you need.

## 8. Protect information

Use City-approved accounts, systems, and access. Follow current requirements
for personnel, legal, security, procurement, resident, and other controlled
information. When unsure, pause and ask the office that owns it.

## 9. Ask for help and share credit

Asking early is usually less costly than repairing avoidable work later. Make
it safe for other people to ask questions too. Name the people who contributed.

## 10. Confirm what happened

Tell the people affected when a decision is made, work is completed, a request
is declined, or the plan changes. Make sure remaining actions have an owner.

## When priorities change

A new request should come with a priority decision. Ask what should stop, move,
or receive less attention.

Staff should not be expected to hide a workload problem through unpaid time,
skipped checks, or silent delay. Managers are responsible for helping resolve
priority conflicts.

## When another requirement controls

Follow current City policy, applicable law, a collective bargaining agreement,
a safety rule, an accommodation, a security requirement, or another formal
instruction that controls the work.

A person may use Human Resources, Equal Employment Opportunity, Ethics, safety,
union, whistleblower, legal, or another applicable formal route directly. These
norms do not require manager approval before using a protected route.
