# Productivity and Collaboration Playbook

{{ page_header(summary="How OPI staff communicate, store shared files, record decisions, and track work.") }}

Use the City-approved tools assigned to your team and the type of information
you handle. This guide does not require a particular software product.

## Choose the right place

| Need | Use |
| --- | --- |
| A quick question or conversation | The team's agreed message or email channel |
| A file several people must edit | One approved shared document |
| Work with an owner and next step | The team's agreed work tracker |
| A decision people will need later | The agreed project or decision record |
| An official, sensitive, or controlled record | The City system named by the office that owns the record |

Before creating a new channel, folder, tracker, or document, ask:

1. Does a current place already exist?
2. Who will maintain the new place?
3. Who needs access?
4. Does the information require special handling?
5. Where will someone look for it later?

If those questions have no clear answers, do not add another place yet.

## Share one copy of a file

When several people need the same document, choose one shared copy and send a
link. Avoid circulating attachments that become competing versions.

Name the document so a person can understand it without opening it. If a file
is still changing, say what kind of review it needs and who will make the next
decision. When a file is ready for review, name the reviewer and next action.

## Make ownership visible

For work that requires follow-through, write down:

- the result needed;
- the person responsible for the next action;
- the next useful date or sequence;
- the reviewer or decision-maker, if there is one; and
- anything blocking the work.

Several people may contribute, but "everyone" is not an owner. One person
should know that they hold the next action.

When new work arrives, ask what should move. Do not hide a capacity problem by
accepting every request and missing several of them later.

## Record decisions

A useful decision note can be short. Capture:

- what was decided;
- who made or authorized the decision;
- why it was chosen;
- what changes next; and
- any question that remains open.

Put the note where the people doing the work can find it. Do not leave the only
record in one person's notes or memory. If a decision changes, update the record
and explain what changed instead of quietly rewriting the past.

## Run useful meetings

A meeting should have a question, decision, review, or working task that
benefits from people being together at the same time.

Before inviting people, provide:

- the purpose;
- what participants should review or prepare;
- who will decide; and
- what the meeting should produce.

During or soon after the meeting, record decisions, owners, and next actions.
Cancel or shorten a meeting when the work can be handled clearly in writing.

## Write for the reader

Start with what the reader needs to know or do. Include context when it changes
the decision or action.

A useful update often covers:

- **Completed:** what changed since the last update;
- **Next:** what will happen next;
- **Blocked:** what needs help; and
- **Decision needed:** who must decide what.

Avoid unexplained initials, office slogans, and status words that do not
describe the actual situation.

## Raise risks while options remain

Say early when work is blocked, a date may move, the evidence is weak, or a
request conflicts with another priority. Name the problem, likely effect, and
help needed. You do not need a complete solution before raising a real problem.

## Protect information

Use only City-approved accounts and storage. Give access based on the work
people need to do. Follow current requirements for personnel, legal,
procurement, security, resident, and other controlled information.

When unsure, pause and ask the office that owns the information. Convenience
does not override privacy, security, records, or legal requirements.

## Confirm what happened

Work is not complete merely because someone sent a message or held a meeting.
Before closing it, confirm that:

- the agreed result exists;
- the right person reviewed or received it;
- open follow-up has an owner;
- the current file is findable; and
- people affected by the outcome know what happens next.

These practices support the [Staff Working Norms](staff-working-norms.md).
