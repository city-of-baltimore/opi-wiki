# New Hire Orientation Guide

{{ page_header(summary="What to expect when you join OPI and how to get help.") }}

This guide explains what to expect before you start, during your first day, and
in your first months at OPI. Your manager will confirm the details that apply to
your job.

City instructions for hiring, pay, benefits, leave, technology, security,
accommodations, and workplace access control. Complete those steps through the
City office that owns them. This guide does not change eligibility, rights, or
deadlines.

## Before your first day

Your manager should tell you:

- when and where to report;
- who will meet you;
- what your first day will include;
- whether you need to bring anything;
- how to request an accommodation or accessibility support; and
- whom to contact if your start plans change.

You may receive separate instructions from Human Resources, payroll, benefits,
security, or BCIT. Do not send sensitive personal information through an
ordinary email or store City records in a personal account.

If an instruction is missing or two instructions conflict, ask the City office
that owns the process. Your manager can help identify that office but should not
guess about formal requirements.

## Your first day

Your first day should help you get oriented. It is not a test of how much you
can absorb.

By the end of the day, you should know:

- who your manager is and how to reach them;
- how to get help with access or technology;
- what OPI does and how your role contributes;
- what your first few priorities are;
- where your team keeps shared work; and
- when you will meet with your manager again.

Complete the official orientation and access steps assigned to you. If an
account, device, badge, or workspace is not ready, tell your manager what is
blocked. You are not responsible for solving an administrative problem by
yourself.

## Your first week

Focus on people, context, and one manageable piece of work.

Your manager should:

- introduce you to the people you will work with most often;
- explain the purpose of your role in plain language;
- show you where current work and decisions are recorded;
- agree with you on a useful first assignment;
- explain how the team asks questions and raises risks; and
- make time for your questions.

Ask for examples of completed work. A real example is often more useful than a
long explanation.

You are not expected to memorize the site. Use these pages to answer questions
that come up in your work.

## Your first month

During your first month, you and your manager should make the job more concrete.
Together, confirm:

- the results you are responsible for;
- which work comes first;
- who reviews or decides on your work;
- what a careful, complete result looks like;
- what decisions you may make yourself;
- what support or training you still need; and
- what should wait when priorities change.

Complete one useful piece of work early enough to receive feedback. The goal is
to learn how the work moves, not to prove that you can work without help.

Keep a short list of access problems, unclear terms, and unanswered questions.
Review it with your manager rather than letting small problems become lasting
barriers.

## Your early months

As you learn the role, your manager should gradually give you clearer
ownership. Regular conversations should cover what is going well, what needs to
change, and whether your priorities fit the time and support available.

If priorities change, ask which earlier work should stop or move. New work
should not be added silently on top of existing commitments.

The [Onboarding Process and Checklist](onboarding-process-and-checklist.md)
gives managers and support staff the companion checklist.

## Where to ask for help

| Question | Start here |
| --- | --- |
| Your work, priorities, or approvals | Your manager or the person leading the work |
| Pay, benefits, leave, hiring records, or accommodations | The current City Human Resources or benefits route |
| Accounts, devices, access, or security | The current BCIT support route |
| Workplace access or office logistics | The office responsible for facilities or administration |
| Conduct, discrimination, ethics, safety, union rights, or another protected concern | The appropriate City, union, or protected-reporting route |

You may use a formal reporting or support route directly when appropriate. This
guide does not require you to raise a protected concern with your manager first.
