# Onboarding Process and Checklist

{{ page_header(summary="A manager's checklist for giving a new employee a clear and supported start.") }}

This checklist is for the manager responsible for onboarding a new OPI
employee. The manager owns the checklist and confirms work assigned to Human
Resources, Baltimore City Information Technology (BCIT), AdminOps, and the
onboarding buddy.

City hiring, pay, benefits, leave, accommodation, security, and technology
requirements control. This checklist helps people coordinate their work; it
does not replace a City process or create a new deadline.

## Who does what

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Manager | Sets the plan, checks readiness, explains the job, resolves gaps, and gives feedback. |
| AdminOps | Coordinates the office, equipment, access, and other administrative requests assigned to it. |
| Human Resources and BCIT | Own their formal personnel, account, security, and technology processes. |
| Onboarding buddy | Answers everyday questions and helps the employee meet colleagues. |
| New employee | Completes assigned City steps, asks questions, and tells the manager what is blocked. |

The manager should always tell the employee who owns an open task and what can
happen while it is unresolved. Do not ask the employee to disclose confidential
medical, benefits, or personnel information to prove that a City process is
complete.

## Before the start date

- Confirm the start date and reporting instructions through the current
  City process.
- Send a welcome message with the first-day time, place, plan, and contact
  for last-minute changes.
- Point the employee to the confidential route for accommodations or
  accessibility support.
- Request only the equipment, accounts, software, workspace, and building
  access required for the job.
- Check each request. Submitting a ticket is not the same as confirming
  readiness.
- Prepare a short first-week schedule with time for setup, introductions,
  questions, and focused work.
- Assign an onboarding buddy when that support would be useful.
- Choose one useful first assignment that can be completed safely even if
  some access is delayed.
- Gather a few current examples of good work.
- Write down the first priorities, likely collaborators, and known
  constraints.

Keep the reading list short. Link to the pages and examples the person needs for
their first work instead of asking them to read the whole site.

## First day

- Welcome the employee and review the day's plan.
- Confirm how to get help with workplace access, technology, and security.
- Explain OPI's purpose and how the employee's role contributes.
- Review the first priorities in plain language.
- Show where the team keeps current shared work.
- Explain who reviews or decides on the first assignment.
- Set the next manager check-in.
- Leave enough time for questions and setup problems.

If access is incomplete, adapt the day. Do not treat an administrative delay as
an employee performance problem.

## First week

- Introduce the employee to the people they will work with most often.
- Explain the difference between the employee's team, the services OPI
  provides, and the work they personally own.
- Walk through one current project from request to decision to follow-up.
- Agree on the first assignment, expected result, reviewer, and next
  check-in.
- Explain how the team records decisions, tracks work, and raises risks.
- Review the [Staff Working Norms](../operations/staff-working-norms.md).
- Use the
  [Productivity and Collaboration Playbook](../operations/productivity-and-collaboration-playbook.md)
  when a question about shared work comes up.
- Ask what remains missing, unclear, or inaccessible.

Only schedule introductory meetings that have a clear use for the person's
job. Explain why each meeting matters.

## First month

- Review the employee's first completed work and give specific feedback.
- Confirm the results, decisions, and relationships the employee owns.
- Identify any remaining access, training, or information gaps.
- Ask whether the workload fits the available time and support.
- Clarify what should stop or move if priorities changed.
- Ask what would have made onboarding easier.
- Improve this checklist or the team's instructions when the answer reveals
  a real gap.

## During the early months

Use regular conversations to adjust the plan, not to manufacture milestones.

At each check-in:

- review priorities and completed work;
- give specific feedback while it can still be used;
- resolve access, workload, and authority problems;
- confirm who reviews or decides on the next work;
- discuss support and learning needs; and
- record the agreements that affect future work.

The employee is well oriented when they can explain what they own, find the
information they need, ask for help without guessing, and complete work with an
appropriate level of review. Finishing a calendar period or attending a list of
meetings is not enough.

Use the [New Hire Orientation Guide](new-hire-orientation-guide.md) as the
employee-facing companion to this checklist.

## When this checklist does not answer the question

Use the City office that owns the issue. Human Resources owns personnel
processes. BCIT owns City technology, accounts, and security. The appropriate
administrative owner handles facilities and purchasing.

Employees may go directly to the applicable Human Resources, Equal Employment
Opportunity, Ethics, safety, union, whistleblower, legal, or other protected
route. Managers must not act as gatekeepers to those routes.
